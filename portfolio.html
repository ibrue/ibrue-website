Smart Glasses Hardware

From concept to mass manufacturing in Shenzhen.

RoleHardware design
StatusPatented · Manufactured
ToolsFusion 360, DFM
OutputProduction tooling

Problem

Smart glasses live or die on weight, balance, and how they sit on a face. Everything — camera, battery, boards, wiring — has to disappear into a frame that still reads as a normal pair of glasses, survives being tossed in a bag, and can be assembled at volume without a skilled technician.

Approach

The frame you're rotating is one of the concept iterations: temple arms sized around the electronics stack, a hinge and side-mount interface that carries load without transferring flex into the optics, and internal channels routing wiring from the arms into the front. Designed around injection-molding draft and snap features tuned for high-volume assembly.

Outcome

  • Concept → DFM → tooling → mass production
  • Co-inventor on patent for power-optimized image capture in smart wearables
  • Worked directly with manufacturing partners in Shenzhen

Differential Cycloidal Gearbox

A new take on an old mechanism — 30% fewer parts.

RoleInventor, designer
StatusPrototyped
ToolsFusion 360, FDM/SLA
Result>30% part reduction

Problem

Cycloidal drives give you huge reduction ratios in a small package with near-zero backlash — which is why robot joints love them. The catch is part count: eccentric bearings, output pins, pin housings, and rollers stack up fast, and every one of them is a tolerance stack and a cost line.

Approach

I reworked the output stage as a differential, letting two cycloidal discs take the reaction load against each other instead of routing it through a separate pin-and-roller assembly. That collapses several components into one and removes an entire tolerance chain.

Outcome

  • Over 30% reduction in part count versus a conventional cycloidal drive
  • Fewer machined interfaces, so it prints and assembles far faster
  • Prototyped in-house on the ENEN Design print farm

2023–2025 — South Dakota State University

JACKS VEX U

Founded a university robotics team from zero.

RoleFounder, lead designer
TeamBuilt from scratch
Funding$50K+ raised
ResultExcellence Award

Problem

SDSU had no VEX U presence. Competing at that level means custom-machined drivetrains, real manufacturing capacity, and travel budget — none of which existed on day one.

Approach

Recruited and trained the founding roster, wrote the sponsorship case, and set the design language for the robots: simple, serviceable subsystems that a student could rebuild between matches. Raised over $50K to fund tooling and travel.

Outcome

  • Excellence Award at the VEX U World Championship — the program's top honor
  • 2nd in the world in skills scoring
  • $50K+ raised; a team that outlives my time on it

Competition

3lb Combat Robots

Designing for the worst day a machine will ever have.

RoleDesign, fabrication
Class3lb beetleweight
FocusImpact survivability
ToolsCAD, CNC, 3D print

Why it matters

Combat robotics is the fastest design feedback loop I've found. Every match is a destructive test with a three-minute report. You learn very quickly which fasteners back out, where stress concentrates, and how much of a design's weight budget is doing nothing.

What I took from it

  • Design for impact loads, not just static loads
  • Serviceability under time pressure is a real design constraint
  • Weight budgets force honesty about what a part is actually for
